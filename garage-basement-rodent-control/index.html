A garage is a rodent's ideal address.

A garage offers what rodents look for: shelter from heat and weather, a floor covered in things to hide behind and materials to shred. It is also checked less often than the kitchen. A problem can run for weeks before anyone looks behind the shelving.

Lower-level rooms, where a home has them, work the same way. Storage rooms and utility areas get used rarely and cluttered often.

What the technician looks at.

  • The garage door corners, bottom seal and the join between door frame and slab.
  • Where plumbing, electric and gas lines enter the wall.
  • Shelving, boxes and bags stacked against walls, especially paper and fabric.
  • The water heater closet, the area behind a freezer and any storage cabinet.
  • Droppings, gnaw marks and nesting material along the baseboard.

Rub marks along the bottom of a wall show regular travel. A trail of droppings from a gap to a stack of boxes usually points to both the way in and the nest.

Wiring and appliances.

Rodents gnaw constantly, because their teeth never stop growing. In a garage, that means cords, hoses, insulation and sometimes vehicle wiring. A freezer cord with exposed wire is a fire risk and a repair item.

Do not touch damaged wiring yourself. Photograph it, switch off the circuit if you know how and it is safe, and tell the technician. Rodent damage repair covers the repair scope.

Closing the garage.

Sealing starts with the door. A bottom seal that has hardened or torn lets mice and small rats in, and so do worn corner seals. Replacing the seals is inexpensive compared with the problem.

Utility penetrations and gaps along the slab come next. We use mesh and sealant that rodents cannot easily chew. Our exclusion service page explains the materials and the order of work.

A housekeeping routine that helps.

Once the animals are gone and the gaps are closed, storage habits decide whether it stays that way. Keep boxes off the floor, leave a gap between stored items and the wall, and check the garage once a month.

If you only occasionally use the garage, a quick look at the door line and the corners each season catches most early signs. A call at the first droppings is cheaper than a call after a nest.

Cooler and hotter months.

A garage that is hot in summer may be avoided by rodents during the day, but they still shelter in cooler corners and behind stored goods. The point is to remove the shelter and the gaps, not to count on weather.

Handle with care: a CDC-based checklist

Read this list before you touch anything that looks like droppings, urine stains or nesting material.

  • Resist the urge to sweep. Dry droppings crumble into dust that is easy to inhale.
  • Before entering a closed space with signs of rodents, open it up and leave for about 30 minutes.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Use a disinfectant on the droppings and the surface under them, wait about five minutes, then wipe with disposable towels.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Before we arrive

  • Clear a path to the attic hatch, the garage wall and the cabinet under the kitchen sink.
  • Leave droppings undisturbed, since protective cleanup is part of the visit.
  • Avoid setting snap traps where children or pets can reach them.
  • Note any odor, since smell can point to where an animal has died.

Garage and lower-level questions

Why do rodents like garages?

They are cluttered, undisturbed and often have gaps at the door or where utilities enter. Stored boxes make good nest material.

Do you work in basements if I have one?

Yes. Lower-level rooms are treated like garages for rodent purposes. Describe the access and what is stored there.

Can rodents chew a freezer cord or a car wire?

Yes. Gnawing on cords and insulation is common, so damage to appliances and vehicles is worth checking.

What should I move before the visit?

Clear a path along the walls if you can. Do not discard anything suspect until the technician has seen it.

Will the garage need sealing?

Often. The door line and utility penetrations are the first places the technician checks.
