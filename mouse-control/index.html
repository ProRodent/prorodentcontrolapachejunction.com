How a mouse problem shows up in Apache Junction homes.

Mice are the rodents most likely to live entirely inside a house. They nest in wall voids, behind appliances, in stored boxes and in the insulation of an attic, and they rarely travel far from food. A single pair can turn into a household problem faster than most people expect.

The signs are small. Droppings are about the size of a grain of rice, gnaw marks appear on food packaging and soft plastic, and a mouse in a wall tends to scratch for a few minutes and then go quiet. Because mice are mostly nocturnal, the first hint is often a sound at night.

A dry desert setting does not prevent this. What a mouse needs is a gap, a food source and a sheltered place to nest, and a house provides all three in any climate.

Finding the route before setting a trap.

A technician starts where the signs are strongest and works outward. Fresh droppings, rub marks and gnawing show the route, and the route shows the opening. Setting traps without that information often catches a few mice and misses the cause.

  • Check under sinks, behind the stove and refrigerator, and around the water heater closet.
  • Look at the garage door corners, the weatherstripping and the wall where it meets the slab.
  • Inspect the attic hatch, the edges of vents and any place where wires or pipes enter.
  • Note stored cardboard, fabric and paper, which mice shred for bedding.

Trapping, then sealing.

Snap traps and enclosed stations are placed along the routes the evidence shows, in places children and pets cannot reach. Mice are curious, so unbaited gaps along walls matter as much as the trap itself.

Sealing starts once the first traps show who is active. Steel wool alone is not enough, because mice pull it out. We use metal flashing, mesh, hardware cloth and sealant, matched to the opening. Our exclusion service page explains the materials.

What not to do while you wait.

Do not sweep up droppings dry, and do not spread loose bait around the house. Dry cleanup lifts dust, and loose poison can end up in a pet or inside a wall where an animal dies. If you want to clean before the visit, follow the wet-cleanup steps in the CDC pages we link on this site.

Food storage helps a lot. Move dry goods and pet food into sealed containers, and take bowls up overnight. That does not solve the problem, but it removes the reward that keeps mice coming back.

After the traps come out.

A good result means no new droppings, no new noise and traps that stay empty. If the attic or a wall void was heavily contaminated, attic and crawl space cleanup is the next step. If wiring or insulation was chewed, rodent damage repair scopes the fix separately.

If you are not sure whether you have mice or something larger, ask for an inspection first. Our inspection and estimates visit names the animal and ends with a plan.

Steps to take before touching droppings or nests

Rodent waste can carry germs, so how you clean matters. The points below summarize public CDC guidance.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests. That can put contaminated dust in the air you breathe.
  • Open doors or vents and let an enclosed space air out for 30 minutes or more before cleanup.
  • Put on gloves before touching anything contaminated, then wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.
  • Soak the droppings and the surface in disinfectant first. Wiping comes only after the soak time.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Symptoms such as fever, body aches or trouble breathing after contact with rodent waste call for medical advice. We cannot assess anyone's health.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets out of any room with droppings, nests or open gaps until the technician has looked.
  • Leave dry droppings alone, because sweeping them raises dust you do not want to breathe.
  • Skip loose bait or poison, which complicates the inspection and can leave animals dying inside walls.
  • Write down when you hear or see activity, since timing helps trace the route.

Mouse control questions

How small a gap can a mouse use?

Roughly a quarter inch, about the width of a pencil. That includes pipe collars, door corners, dryer vents and gaps where wires enter a wall.

Are mice a problem in a desert climate?

House mice live indoors wherever they find food and shelter, so a dry climate does not keep them out. Heat and monsoon rain can push them toward cooler, drier buildings.

Can I use poison from a store?

Consumer products are restricted to ready-to-use bait stations, and poison can leave animals dying inside walls. Ask the technician before you use anything.

How long until the noises stop?

It depends on the number of mice and whether the entry points are sealed. The technician gives a realistic expectation after the inspection.

Do you take mouse calls at night?

Yes. The line is answered around the clock and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.
