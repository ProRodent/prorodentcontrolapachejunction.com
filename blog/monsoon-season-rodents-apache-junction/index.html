Monsoon Season and Rodents in Apache Junction, AZ.

Arizona's monsoon season runs from mid-June through September. Storms change where animals look for shelter, and a short checklist can help.

Arizona's monsoon season officially runs from mid-June through September. It brings humidity, wind and heavy rain, usually in short, intense storms. These storms change the landscape quickly, and they can change where small animals look for shelter.

What a storm can change for rodents.

We cannot say how many animals move because of a storm, and nobody can promise it. What is well understood is that heavy rain can flood burrows and dens and soak nesting material. Animals that lose their shelter look for another, and a house, garage or vehicle can look like a dry place.

Storms also move debris, knock branches onto roofs and create gaps. Damaged flashing or a loosened vent can become a new way in.

Before the storms.

  • Walk the roofline and check vents, flashing and tile or shingle edges.
  • Trim branches and fronds that touch the roof so they cannot bridge to it.
  • Clear debris from the base of walls and from under stored material.
  • Seal gaps at garage door corners and utility entries.
  • Store pet food and birdseed in sealed containers.

After a storm.

When the rain stops and it is safe, look around. A few minutes can catch problems early.

Open ground and washes.

If your lot borders open ground, storm runoff may change the lot edge. Soil can erode, brush can pile up and burrows can be exposed. Mention the lot edge on the call, and the technician will check it. We do not claim that your property is more or less at risk. We look and report.

What not to do in the storm period.

  • Do not climb on a wet or hot roof to inspect it.
  • Do not plug a hole while an animal may be inside.
  • Do not spread loose bait where rain, pets or children can reach it.
  • Do not sweep up droppings dry.

Federal rules limit consumer rodenticides to bait stations, and the EPA page in our sources explains why. Rain also spreads loose poison, which is a risk to pets and wildlife.

A realistic plan.

You do not have to treat monsoon as an emergency. Treat it as a good moment for a short walk-around, one before and one after the season. If you find signs, call and describe them. Rodent exclusion closes the gaps and inspection and estimates covers the first visit.

Drainage, debris and standing water.

Storm runoff leaves behind wet leaves, branches and trash that can pile up against walls. That debris is shelter. Clear it back from the base of the house once it is dry, and check places where water pools near the foundation.

If you see a burrow that appeared after rain, photograph it and keep pets away. Do not fill it yourself without advice, because blocking a burrow can push an animal toward the house.

If you hear something during a storm.

Noise in the ceiling during a storm can be thunder, wind, a loose roof piece or an animal. Wait until it is safe, then listen again at night, and note the time and place. The line is open 24/7 if you want to talk it through.

What to do, and not do, while you wait

If you plan to clean before a visit, these CDC-based points are the safe way to do it. If you are unsure, wait and ask first.

  • Never dry sweep or vacuum droppings, urine stains or nesting material.
  • Give the room time to ventilate. Thirty minutes with the doors open is the common recommendation.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Wet first, wipe second. Disinfectant needs about five minutes on the surface before you remove anything.
  • Waste goes into a sealed bag, not an open bin. Use gloves or a tool for any dead animal.
  • A fever or trouble breathing after rodent cleanup deserves a call to your doctor, with the exposure mentioned up front.

Reader questions

When is monsoon season?

In Arizona it runs from mid-June through September. Storms vary a great deal from year to year and place to place.

Do storms really push rodents into houses?

Heavy rain can flood burrows and nesting areas, and animals looking for dry shelter may move. It is a plausible factor, not a certainty.

Should I check the attic after a storm?

Look at the roof edges and vents from outside, and check the attic for new droppings or noises. Do not go up in extreme heat.

Does standing water matter?

Standing water and wet debris near the house can attract animals. Drain and clear them where you can.

Is monsoon the only risky season?

No. Summer heat and cooler weather both change behavior, and a house with open gaps is a risk all year.
