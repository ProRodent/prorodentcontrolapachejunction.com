Safe Cleanup of Rodent Droppings at Home: A CDC-Based Guide.

Cleaning rodent waste the wrong way can put dust in the air you breathe. The CDC describes a safer method, and this guide restates it plainly.

Rodent droppings, urine and nesting material can carry germs. Dry waste can crumble into fine dust, which is easy to stir up and breathe. That is why the way you clean matters as much as whether you clean.

This guide restates the cleanup advice on the CDC pages linked at the bottom. It is general public health information, and it is not medical advice.

Step one: ventilate first.

If the space has been closed up, such as an attic, garage, shed or storage room, open it and let it air out for about thirty minutes before you work. Stay out of it while it airs. In summer, do not go into a hot attic to do this at midday.

Step two: protect yourself.

  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves.
  • Consider a respirator or mask rated for the job if you are working in an enclosed space.
  • Keep children, pets and others out of the area.
  • Wear clothes you can wash afterward.

Gloves are the minimum. If you are not comfortable doing this, or the area is large or hard to reach, ask for help.

Step three: wet, do not sweep.

Spray droppings, urine stains and nesting material with disinfectant or a diluted bleach solution until they are thoroughly wet. Let the disinfectant sit for about five minutes. Do not sweep or vacuum dry waste.

Wiping with paper towels is the next move. Pick up the wet material and the towels, and put them straight into a bag.

Step four: clean the area and yourself.

After the waste is removed, mop or sponge the floor and surfaces with disinfectant. Clean any items that were contaminated. Wipe down gloves with disinfectant or soap and water before you take them off, then wash your hands thoroughly.

  • Wash hands with soap and water after you remove gloves.
  • Wash clothes that may have been contaminated.
  • Seal and discard cleaning materials that cannot be disinfected.

Dead rodents and traps.

A dead rodent should be handled with gloves. Place it in a bag, seal the bag and put it in the trash. Disinfect the trap and the area before resetting. Do not handle live rodents.

When to call a professional.

Call for help when the area is large, hard to reach, very hot or heavily contaminated. Attics and crawl spaces fit that description more often than not. Attic and crawl space rodent cleanup describes how it is done, and rodent exclusion comes first so animals cannot return.

Common mistakes to avoid.

  • Sweeping or vacuuming dry droppings, which lifts dust into the air.
  • Skipping gloves because the area looks clean.
  • Wiping before the disinfectant has had time to sit.
  • Reusing sponges or rags that touched droppings.
  • Throwing waste into an open bin instead of a sealed bag.

If you are unsure at any step, stop and ask. A short pause is better than guessing, and the CDC pages linked here explain each step in detail.

Your health.

If you feel unwell in the weeks after cleaning, with fever, muscle aches or trouble breathing, tell a doctor that you were exposed to rodent waste. We cannot give health advice, and we do not judge anyone's individual risk.

Before you sweep, spray or bag anything

General safety practice drawn from CDC guidance on rodents. It is meant to keep you safe before any professional arrives.

  • Dry sweeping is the mistake to avoid. It lifts fine dust from droppings and nests into the room.
  • Give the room time to ventilate. Thirty minutes with the doors open is the common recommendation.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Saturate the droppings and nearby surfaces with disinfectant. After about five minutes, take up the waste with paper towels.
  • Seal everything you wipe up in a bag before it goes in the trash, and do not handle carcasses bare-handed.
  • Tell a physician about any rodent exposure if flu-like symptoms or breathing problems follow. We are a pest service and cannot diagnose anything.

Quick answers

Can I use a vacuum if it has a filter?

The CDC advises against vacuuming dry droppings. A wet method is the safer approach.

What disinfectant should I use?

The CDC describes disinfectant or a diluted bleach solution. Follow product instructions and ventilate.

How long should the disinfectant sit?

About five minutes before you wipe, according to CDC guidance.

Should I hire someone for a large area?

For an attic, crawl space or a large amount of waste, many people do. It is hot, cramped work.

When should I see a doctor?

If you develop fever, aches or trouble breathing in the weeks after cleanup, tell a doctor about the exposure.
