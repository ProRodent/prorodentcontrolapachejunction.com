Noises in the Attic or Walls at Night: Mouse, Rat or Packrat?.

Night noises are often the first sign of a rodent problem. Sound alone cannot name the animal, but it gives useful clues.

Most people first learn about rodents at night. A faint scratching in the ceiling, a patter in a wall, a thump over the bedroom. This guide helps you describe what you hear, and know what to do next.

What different sounds can suggest.

Sound is a clue, not a diagnosis. Even experienced technicians confirm with droppings, rub marks and entry points. But what you hear helps decide what to look for.

What you hear.Possible cause.Next step.
Light scratching, quick and short.Mice in a wall or attic.Look for small droppings and gnawing.
Heavier thumping, running overhead.Rats or larger animals.Check the attic hatch and roof edges.
Rustling in a cabinet.A mouse in a kitchen.Check under sinks and behind appliances.
Gnawing or chewing sounds.Any rodent.Look for chewed edges and wiring.
Occasional ticking or popping.Heating, cooling and expansion.Not usually a rodent.

Notes worth making before you call.

  • The time of night and how long it lasts.
  • Which room or wall the sound seems to come from.
  • Whether it moves along a path or stays in one place.
  • Whether it happens every night or only some.
  • Whether it started after rain, a storm or work on the roof.

Those notes help a technician decide where to start. They also help rule out noises that are not rodents.

Check from the outside.

While you are listening, check the outside of the house in daylight. Look at vents, roof edges and places where wires and pipes enter. Check for gnawed edges, rub marks and droppings. Look at trees and fronds that touch the roof.

Do not climb on the roof or into the attic if conditions are unsafe, especially in the heat.

Safe steps while you wait.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum droppings dry.
  • Keep pets and children out of any room with droppings.
  • Do not spread loose bait or poison.
  • Do not seal holes that an animal may still be inside.

Other animals that can cause noises.

Not every sound is a mouse or a rat. Squirrels are active in daylight, birds can nest in vents, and pipes and ducts expand and contract as the temperature changes. A technician will tell you honestly when the evidence points to something outside what we handle.

  • Daylight thumping suggests an animal that is active by day, not a mouse or rat.
  • Chirping or fluttering near a vent suggests birds.
  • Ticking and popping that follows temperature changes is usually the building itself.
  • Noises plus droppings, gnawing or rub marks point toward rodents.

How soon to call.

If you hear it more than one night, call. If you also see droppings or gnawing, call the same day. Waiting lets a small problem grow.

A short safety list before anyone cleans up

Short, practical safety points based on CDC cleanup guidance. They are not medical advice.

  • Do not use a vacuum on rodent waste, even a strong one. Wet it down first, as the steps below describe.
  • A space that has been closed for weeks should air out for at least 30 minutes before you work in it.
  • Gloves on first, handwashing last. Do both every time.
  • Spray the area with disinfectant, wait roughly five minutes, then wipe it up with paper towels instead of dry material.
  • Close the bag tightly before disposal. A dead rodent should only be handled with protection on your hands.
  • We cannot offer health advice. If symptoms appear after cleaning rodent waste, see a doctor and explain what you handled.

Reader questions

Can I tell a mouse from a rat by sound?

Sometimes. Light, quick scratching often suggests a smaller animal, and heavier thumping suggests a larger one. Sound alone is not reliable.

Could it be something other than a rodent?

Yes. Squirrels, birds, bats, pipes, heating and cooling expansion and wind can all make noise.

Why are sounds louder at night?

Rodents are mostly active after dark, and the house is quieter, so small sounds carry.

Should I knock on the wall?

It is better not to. Knocking can send the animal elsewhere and tells you little.

What if the sounds stop?

That can mean the animal moved or is resting. Droppings and gnawing are more reliable evidence.
