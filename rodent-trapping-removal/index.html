Trapping is placement, not volume.

Scattering a dozen traps around a house rarely works. Rodents follow edges, avoid open floor and test anything new. A trap in the right place, along the route they already use, will catch more than many in the wrong spots.

That is why trapping comes after inspection. The technician needs to see where droppings and rub marks line up before choosing a spot.

Checking, clearing and adjusting.

A trap is not a set-and-forget item. It gets checked at an agreed interval, cleared and reset, and moved if it is not producing. Checking regularly keeps an animal from suffering and keeps the smell of a dead rodent from becoming a second problem.

  • Wear gloves and treat anything caught as contaminated.
  • Double bag the animal and the used paper towels, then seal the bag.
  • Disinfect the surface before resetting the trap.
  • Wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.

The steps match the CDC cleanup guidance linked on this page. If you would rather not handle traps, tell the technician and the check schedule can be handled for you.

Why traps alone are not the end.

Trapping clears the animals that are inside. If every opening stays open, the next animals arrive within days. The plan should always include exclusion and entry point sealing to keep the result.

Poison is a different path. Consumer bait is limited to bait stations under federal rules, and a poisoned animal can die in an inaccessible space. We prefer traps because we know where the animal is.

If your lot borders open ground.

Some properties in Apache Junction sit next to open land, and some have a shed or a garage that holds stored goods. Trap placement outdoors depends on where activity was seen, and other desert wildlife can cross a lot. Covered stations keep the focus on rodents and away from anything else.

Before you call: safe steps for rodent signs

General safety practice drawn from CDC guidance on rodents. It is meant to keep you safe before any professional arrives.

  • No broom, no vacuum, no blower. Dry methods spread whatever is in the droppings.
  • Open vents, doors or hatches first and wait about half an hour before anyone starts cleaning.
  • Gloves are the minimum. Wash them while still on your hands, then wash your hands after taking them off.
  • Soak, wait about five minutes, then wipe. That order keeps dust down and gives the disinfectant time to work.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • Flu-like symptoms or shortness of breath after rodent exposure are a reason to see a doctor and mention the cleanup. Health questions belong with a physician, not a pest company.

Trapping questions

How many traps will you use?

That depends on how much activity there is. A few well-placed traps often work better than many scattered ones.

Are glue boards used?

Glue boards are discouraged because they are hard on pets and can cause animals to suffer. Ask what is planned before work starts.

What happens to the trapped animals?

Dead animals are handled with gloves and disposed of safely. Ask about live capture if you want it considered.

How long does trapping take?

Small mouse jobs can clear in days, while larger or older problems take longer. The technician gives a range after the inspection.

Do traps work without sealing?

Not for long. New animals arrive through the same gaps unless the openings are closed.
