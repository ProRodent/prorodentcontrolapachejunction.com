Staying safe while you wait.

The most important rule is to avoid stirring up droppings. The CDC advises against sweeping or vacuuming them dry, because that lifts dust into the air. Wetting them with disinfectant first is the safer method.

  • Keep pets and visitors out of rooms with droppings or nests.
  • Do not climb into an attic yourself if heat or footing is a concern.
  • Photograph droppings, gnawing or a nest and leave them in place.
  • Call a doctor if fever or breathing problems follow exposure, and mention it.

We are not medical professionals and cannot judge anyone's health risk. The CDC links at the bottom of the page explain the cleanup steps.

Desert yards and vehicles, if they apply to you.

If your home has a yard with gravel, plantings or palms, ask the technician to look at them, because plants that touch a roof can give rodents a way up. If you park a vehicle in a carport or garage, check under the hood now and then. Packrats, also called woodrats, are known to build nests in engine bays.

These are things to check, not things we claim about your community. Our vehicle rodent removal and backyard and outdoor rodent control pages explain both.

Safe handling checklist for droppings and nests

These steps follow CDC guidance on cleaning up after rodents. They are general safety practice for any household.

  • Do not use a vacuum on rodent waste, even a strong one. Wet it down first, as the steps below describe.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Put on gloves before touching anything contaminated, then wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.
  • Spray the area with disinfectant, wait roughly five minutes, then wipe it up with paper towels instead of dry material.
  • Bag the waste, seal the bag, and put it in the trash. Do not handle live or dead rodents with bare hands.
  • Watch your health for several weeks after a cleanup. If symptoms like fever or breathlessness appear, tell your doctor about the rodent exposure.

Before we arrive

  • Keep dogs and cats indoors or leashed if the activity is in the yard or garage.

  • Do not plug a hole yourself first, because sealing it with an animal inside creates new problems.
