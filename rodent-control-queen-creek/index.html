Steps to take before touching droppings or nests

Short, practical safety points based on CDC cleanup guidance. They are not medical advice.

  • Resist the urge to sweep. Dry droppings crumble into dust that is easy to inhale.
  • A space that has been closed for weeks should air out for at least 30 minutes before you work in it.
  • Use disposable or washable rubber gloves, and wash both gloves and hands when you are done.
  • Mist droppings with a disinfectant or bleach solution so nothing lifts into the air, and let it sit about five minutes.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Symptoms such as fever, body aches or trouble breathing after contact with rodent waste call for medical advice. We cannot assess anyone's health.
