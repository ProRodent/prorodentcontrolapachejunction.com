Why cleanup is its own step.

Removing rodents and cleaning up after them are different jobs. Trapping and sealing deal with the animals. Cleanup deals with what stays behind: droppings, urine, nesting material and soiled insulation.

That material can carry germs, and it holds odor. It can also attract new rodents, because it carries the scent of a past nest. Cleanup is part of making the space safe and part of keeping it unattractive.

What the CDC says about the method.

The CDC advises against sweeping or vacuuming dry droppings and nests, because that lifts dust into the air. It recommends ventilating the space, wearing gloves, wetting droppings with disinfectant and wiping them up with paper towels. The links are on this page.

  • Air the space out for about thirty minutes before anyone works in it.
  • Wear gloves and appropriate protection, and wash up thoroughly afterward.
  • Wet droppings and nests with disinfectant before removing anything.
  • Bag waste, seal the bag and dispose of it with household trash.

Heat is a real factor in Apache Junction summers. An attic can be far hotter than the outside air, so cleanup is planned for cooler hours and with rest breaks. If you can wait until the weather cools, that is easier on everyone, though it should not delay sealing.

What gets removed and what stays.

Insulation that is lightly soiled may be treated, while heavily soiled or nested-in material is removed. The technician shows you what is contaminated and explains why. You decide what to replace, and the estimate lists new insulation separately.

If wiring or ducts were chewed, they are flagged for repair instead of cleaned. See rodent damage repair for that scope.

Order of work.

The sequence matters. First the animals are removed, then the entry points are sealed, and only then is the space cleaned. Cleaning before sealing wastes money, because new animals can undo it.

If you are not yet sure what is living in the attic, start with an inspection. If an animal already died up there, see dead rodent removal and odor control.

A note for homes near open land.

Properties close to the Superstition foothills or open desert may have animals that use the roofline and attic as shelter, especially in the hottest and wettest months. That is worth mentioning on the call, because it affects how long the cleanup may take and whether to expect repeat visits.

Before you sweep, spray or bag anything

The CDC publishes cleanup steps for homes with rodent droppings or nests. This list restates the main points in plain words.

  • Treat dry droppings as something to dampen first, not something to brush up.
  • Do not walk straight into a sealed storage area. Open it, leave, and come back after about 30 minutes.
  • Protect your hands with gloves, then wash up carefully even if the gloves look clean.
  • Use a disinfectant on the droppings and the surface under them, wait about five minutes, then wipe with disposable towels.
  • Dispose of wipes and droppings in a sealed plastic bag, and keep skin off dead animals.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Cleanup questions

Do I need cleanup after the rodents are gone?

Often yes. Droppings, urine and nesting material remain and can carry germs and odor, so the space should be cleaned.

Can I clean the attic myself?

You can, if you follow CDC steps and wear proper protection. Many people prefer to hire it out because attics are hot and cramped.

Does insulation have to be replaced?

Heavily soiled insulation is often removed. The technician will show you what is contaminated and what is fine.

Is cleanup done in summer heat?

Attics can be dangerously hot in summer, so work is scheduled for cooler hours and with breaks.

How long does it take?

It depends on the size of the space and how much needs removing. The technician gives an estimate after looking.
