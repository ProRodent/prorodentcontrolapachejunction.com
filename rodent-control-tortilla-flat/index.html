A very small place with a long history.

Tortilla Flat is an unincorporated community in Maricopa County, and the 2020 census counted six residents. It is known as the last surviving stagecoach stop along the Apache Trail, and it began as a freight camp during the 1904 construction of Roosevelt Dam. If you own a building there, call so we can plan the inspection and trap placement.

None of that history explains rodent activity, and we do not claim it does. What it does tell you is that this is a small, remote place where visits are planned in advance. If you own or run a building there, call early, describe how it is used and let us plan the trip.

Before you sweep, spray or bag anything

Read this list before you touch anything that looks like droppings, urine stains or nesting material.

  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests. That can put contaminated dust in the air you breathe.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Hand protection matters. Wear gloves throughout and wash your hands with soap afterward.
  • Soak the droppings and the surface in disinfectant first. Wiping comes only after the soak time.
  • Waste goes into a sealed bag, not an open bin. Use gloves or a tool for any dead animal.
  • If you develop fever, muscle aches or trouble breathing in the weeks after cleaning up rodent droppings, tell a doctor about the exposure. We are not medical professionals and cannot judge individual health risk.
