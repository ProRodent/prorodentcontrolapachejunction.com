Damage is part of the problem.

Rodents gnaw constantly to wear their teeth down, and that gnawing damages what is nearby. Insulation is shredded, ducts are chewed through, screens and weatherstripping are torn, and wire coverings are stripped.

Removal and sealing stop the damage. Repair undoes it. They are separate jobs and are quoted separately, so you can see the cost of each.

Wiring is a safety matter.

Gnawed wiring can short and start a fire. If you see exposed copper, a burned smell or an outlet that is warm, treat it as an emergency. Turn off the circuit if you know how and it is safe, and call an electrician.

We flag what we see and point you to the right trade. We do not do electrical repair, and we will not suggest you ignore wiring damage.

Why repair comes after sealing.

If gaps are still open, a repair can be chewed again. Fresh insulation or new drywall is no more resistant than the old. Sealing first protects the investment.

  • Remove the animals and confirm the traps are clear.
  • Close the entry points with metal, mesh and sealant.
  • Clean and disinfect where needed.
  • Repair or replace damaged materials.

That sequence appears throughout our service pages. See rodent exclusion for sealing, and attic and crawl space cleanup for the step before repair.

Documenting damage.

Take photos before anything is moved, and write down what you see and when. Keep any receipts. If you plan to ask your insurer, they will want to know the date and extent of the damage.

We can describe what we found on the visit. Insurance decisions are the insurer's, and we do not say what a policy will cover.

How long repairs take.

Small repairs may take a short visit. Larger ones, such as replacing a section of attic insulation or a length of duct, take longer. The estimate gives a time range.

Heat is a consideration in summer, since attic work is hard in high temperatures. Work can be scheduled for cooler hours.

Steps to take before touching droppings or nests

This is general public-health guidance from the CDC, not a description of any one job. It applies whether you call us or anyone else.

  • Avoid any dry cleanup of droppings or nests, because disturbed waste turns into airborne dust.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Gloves are the minimum. Wash them while still on your hands, then wash your hands after taking them off.
  • Soak, wait about five minutes, then wipe. That order keeps dust down and gives the disinfectant time to work.
  • Seal everything you wipe up in a bag before it goes in the trash, and do not handle carcasses bare-handed.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Before we arrive

Repair questions

Do you repair electrical wiring?

Exposed or damaged wiring is flagged for a qualified electrician, and we recommend you do not touch it.

What can be repaired directly?

Typical items are insulation, small drywall holes, ducting covers, screens and vent covers. The technician confirms what is in scope.

Does insurance cover rodent damage?

Policies differ, and we cannot say what yours covers. Photographs and a written description of the damage help if you ask your insurer.

Should I repair before sealing?

No. Repairs done before the gaps are closed can be chewed again.

Can damage cause a fire?

Gnawed wiring can short, and that is a fire risk. Treat damaged wiring as an emergency and call an electrician.
