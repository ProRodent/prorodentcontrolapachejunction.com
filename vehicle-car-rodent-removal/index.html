Why a vehicle looks like a shelter.

An engine bay is warm after a drive, enclosed and often undisturbed for days. To a small animal it resembles a den. Cabin air filters and intake boxes add soft material and dark corners.

A vehicle that sits for a week or two, such as a second car or a seasonal vehicle, gives rodents time to settle in. The longer it sits, the more comfortable they get.

Packrats and engine bays.

Packrats, also called woodrats, are a native desert group known for gathering sticks, cactus pieces and other debris into a nest. They are known to use sheltered places such as engine compartments and the spaces beside the engine. If you find a pile of stems or plant debris under the hood, a packrat is a likely suspect, though only inspection can confirm it.

Mice and rats can do similar damage. The nest material differs: mice shred soft items into fine bedding, while a packrat's nest is coarser and built from larger pieces.

What gets checked.

  • The engine bay, including the area around the battery, belts and hoses.
  • The air filter box and the cabin air filter housing.
  • Wiring harnesses, where insulation is often gnawed.
  • The underside of the vehicle and the areas around the wheel wells.
  • The parking spot, including the garage corners and nearby shelter.

Chewed wire is the biggest concern. Exposed conductors can short and cause a fire, and a repair is a job for a mechanic. We remove the animals and nests and tell you where we saw damage.

Where the vehicle is parked.

Removing a nest does not stop the next animal from using the vehicle. The technician looks at the spot where the vehicle sits, whether that is a driveway, a carport or a garage. Food, stored boxes, bird feeders and plantings near the parking spot all matter.

A closed garage helps, but only if the garage is sealed. For that, see garage and basement rodent control. For burrows near the parking area, backyard and outdoor rodent control applies.

Heat, parking and habits.

A vehicle parked in shade is more comfortable for a rodent than one in full sun. After a drive in summer the engine bay stays warm for a while, which draws animals at night. Opening the hood and glancing inside once a week costs nothing.

Do not use mothballs or poison in or around the vehicle, because they can harm pets and children and do not reliably deter rodents.

Before you call: safe steps for rodent signs

This is general public-health guidance from the CDC, not a description of any one job. It applies whether you call us or anyone else.

  • Never dry sweep or vacuum droppings, urine stains or nesting material.
  • Do not walk straight into a sealed storage area. Open it, leave, and come back after about 30 minutes.
  • Put on gloves before touching anything contaminated, then wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.
  • Dampen droppings and urine stains with disinfectant or diluted bleach, leave it for about five minutes, then wipe with paper towels.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • We cannot offer health advice. If symptoms appear after cleaning rodent waste, see a doctor and explain what you handled.

Before we arrive

  • Close the door on the busiest room if you can and keep foot traffic out of it.
  • Stay out of the attic until after the inspection, since disturbed insulation spreads dust.
  • Do not prod nests or burrows, which tends to scatter animals into new areas.
  • Take phone photos of droppings, gnawing or damage so the technician can see what you saw.

Vehicle questions

Why would a rodent nest in a car?

An engine bay is sheltered and warm after a drive, and a vehicle that sits for days is undisturbed. Soft debris and insulation make easy bedding.

Are packrats a vehicle concern?

Packrats, also called woodrats, are known for building nests of sticks and debris in sheltered spots, including engine compartments. The technician will tell you what the nest material suggests.

Is it safe to start the vehicle?

If you see nest material or smell burning, do not start it until it has been checked. Chewed wires can cause shorts and fires.

Do you repair the wiring?

We remove nests and animals and flag damage. A mechanic or auto electrician repairs vehicle wiring.

How do I keep it from happening again?

Park in a closed garage where possible, remove food, keep the area clear and check under the hood now and then.
