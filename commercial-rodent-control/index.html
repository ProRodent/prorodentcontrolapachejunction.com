Why businesses need a plan, not a one-off.

A home can live with a trap for a few days. A business that serves customers or stores goods cannot. A sighting can lead to a complaint or a closure, and gnawed stock is a direct loss.

A plan has three parts: finding and closing the openings, monitoring for new activity and keeping records. Each visit builds on the last.

What gets inspected.

  • Doors, thresholds, loading areas and any gap along the dock or delivery entry.
  • Kitchens, prep areas, storerooms and dry storage.
  • Waste areas, dumpsters and the way lids and doors are kept.
  • Roof access, rooftop equipment and utility penetrations.
  • Shared walls, ceilings and any voids between units.

Rodents come in with deliveries, through doors left open and through gaps at the building edge. The technician looks at the way the business actually runs, not just the building.

Visitors, deliveries and busy seasons.

Goldfield Ghost Town was established in 1893 and is now a revived tourist attraction, and the Superstition Mountain Museum is another place visitors go. Any business that sees visitor traffic, deliveries and open doors has more chances for rodents to enter, so plan the door routine and the waste routine ahead of busy periods and call us for a check.

Cleanup safety, drawn from CDC guidance

Read this list before you touch anything that looks like droppings, urine stains or nesting material.

  • Do not use a vacuum on rodent waste, even a strong one. Wet it down first, as the steps below describe.
  • Let fresh air move through a stuffy garage or storage room for half an hour before you start.
  • Wear rubber or plastic gloves for the whole job, and scrub your hands with soap and water at the end.
  • Wet droppings with a disinfectant or a bleach solution and let it soak for about five minutes before wiping with paper towels.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • Tell a physician about any rodent exposure if flu-like symptoms or breathing problems follow. We are a pest service and cannot diagnose anything.

Before we arrive

  • Keep dogs and cats indoors or leashed if the activity is in the yard or garage.
  • Do not pour cleaners over droppings before the inspection.
  • Do not plug a hole yourself first, because sealing it with an animal inside creates new problems.
  • Remember where you saw the animal and which direction it ran.
