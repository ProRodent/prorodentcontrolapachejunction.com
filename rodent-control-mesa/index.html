Different buildings, different plans.

A big city has many building types, and rodent work differs between them. A detached house means attic, garage and yard. A townhome or apartment adds shared walls. A business adds deliveries and records.

  • Detached homes: roofline, attic, garage and yard checks.
  • Townhomes and apartments: shared walls and coordination with management.
  • Businesses: doors, storage, waste and written visit notes.

What people call about.

Descriptions change, but the pattern is familiar. Someone hears a noise at night, finds droppings, notices a smell or sees a rodent in daylight. In vehicles, the report is often a warning light or chewed wiring.

  • Scratching in a wall or ceiling after dark.
  • Droppings in cabinets, garages or storage.
  • A rodent seen indoors or on a fence at dusk.
  • A smell that suggests an animal died in a wall.

Shared walls and property managers.

If you rent, tell your landlord or manager before or while you call. Rodents move through voids between units, and a plan that stops at one door often fails. Sealing and trapping work best when neighbors act together.

Keep your own records: dates, photos and what you reported. They help if a dispute arises, though what a lease requires is a question for your landlord or an attorney, not for us.

How the visit goes.

The technician inspects, explains the findings and gives a plan and price. Trapping and sealing follow. Our inspection and estimates page explains the first visit in more detail.

What to do, and not do, while you wait

Read this list before you touch anything that looks like droppings, urine stains or nesting material.

  • Avoid any dry cleanup of droppings or nests, because disturbed waste turns into airborne dust.
  • Do not walk straight into a sealed storage area. Open it, leave, and come back after about 30 minutes.
  • Use disposable or washable rubber gloves, and wash both gloves and hands when you are done.
  • Saturate the droppings and nearby surfaces with disinfectant. After about five minutes, take up the waste with paper towels.
  • Seal used paper towels and any dead rodent in a bag before disposal. Keep bare skin out of it.
  • A fever or trouble breathing after rodent cleanup deserves a call to your doctor, with the exposure mentioned up front.
