How a visit works.

The same four steps apply to a mouse in a kitchen cabinet and a packrat nest in an engine bay.

  1. 1

    Inspect.

    A technician walks the attic, garage, yard and roofline to find fresh activity and every opening the animals are using.

  2. 2

    Treat.

    Traps and stations go along the routes the evidence points to, matched to the animal and kept away from children and pets.

  3. 3

    Seal.

    Gaps, vents and gnawed openings are closed with metal, mesh and sealant that rodents cannot chew through.

  4. 4

    Follow up.

    We return to check the traps, confirm the seals are holding and talk through cleanup and repairs.

Signs you may have rodents.

Two or more of these in the same part of the house usually means the activity is current.

  • Droppings.

    Dark pellets along baseboards, in drawers, on garage shelves or on attic insulation.

  • Gnawing.

    Fresh gnaw marks look pale and rough on wood, plastic pipe, wiring and food packaging.

  • Night noises.

    Scratching, scurrying or thumping in a wall or ceiling, mostly after dark.

  • Nest material.

    Shredded paper, insulation, fabric or plant debris packed into a hidden corner.

  • Rub marks.

    Greasy smudges along walls, pipes and beams where animals travel the same path.

  • Odor.

    A musty or ammonia-like smell in a closed room, or a sharper smell where an animal died.

Rodent control with the desert edge in mind

Apache Junction sits against the Superstition Mountains, which bound the city on the east and are federal wilderness. That means a lot of properties here share a boundary with open ground, and open ground is where many rodents live before they ever find a house.

Which animal you have changes the plan. House mice live inside, and a gap about a quarter inch wide is enough for one. Rats need roughly half an inch. Packrats, also called woodrats, are a native desert group that builds nests from sticks and debris and will move into a car engine bay.

Roof rats climb. If your yard has palms or citrus, an animal can travel along a branch or a block wall straight to the roofline. We trace that route during the inspection and say what we find in plain terms before any work starts.

Why the phone line stays open all night.

Rodents are mostly active after dark, so most people first notice them at night. The line is answered around the clock, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows. Describe what you heard, saw or smelled, and say which room it was in.

Summer is hard on attics and vehicles, and the monsoon months from mid-June through September can bring heavy rain. Both can push animals toward shade, water and shelter, so a gap that went unused in spring may start to matter.

  • Walk the outside of the house once a season and look at vents, weep holes and pipe penetrations.
  • Keep pet food and birdseed in sealed containers instead of open bowls or feeders.
  • Pull woodpiles, pallets and stacked boxes a few feet away from walls.
  • Keep trimmed branches off the roof so they cannot act as a bridge.

Questions about rodent control in Apache Junction

How can I tell if I have mice, rats or packrats?

Size and place give clues. Small droppings in drawers and cabinets point to mice, larger droppings along rooflines and in attics point to rats, and a pile of sticks and debris near a wall or in an engine bay suggests a packrat. The inspection confirms it.

How do rodents get into homes here?

Mice can use gaps about a quarter inch wide and rats roughly half an inch. Pipe and wire penetrations, vents, garage door corners and roof edges are the usual routes.

Will traps alone solve the problem?

Trapping removes the animals that are inside, but sealing the openings stops new ones from arriving. Most jobs involve both, in that order.

Is the treatment safe around children and pets?

Tell the technician about every child and pet before work starts. Traps and stations are placed out of reach, and the technician explains what was used and where.

How quickly can someone come out?

The line is answered around the clock and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows. Call, describe the problem and ask what is open today.

What happens after the first visit?

We check the traps, confirm the seals are holding and advise on cleanup. If damage is found, repair is scoped as its own step.
