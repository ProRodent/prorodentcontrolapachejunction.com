Rats are a different problem from mice.

A rat is larger, stronger and more cautious than a mouse. It gnaws through softer materials, carries more nesting material and covers more ground. That changes where we look and what we use.

Roof rats are climbers. They run along fences, wires, tree limbs and the top of block walls, and they enter at the roofline instead of the foundation. If you hear thumping overhead around dusk, a climbing rat is one possibility the inspection will rule in or out.

Reading the yard and the roofline.

The inspection begins outside. A technician looks at what touches the roof, what grows near the walls and where food might be available. Palms and citrus are worth mentioning on the call, because dropped fruit and dense fronds can feed and shelter rats.

  • Branches or fronds that touch or overhang the roof.
  • Fruit or nuts left to rot on the ground.
  • Pet food, birdseed or a feeder within reach of the wall.
  • Gaps at the roof edge, soffit vents, gable vents and rooftop pipe penetrations.

Open ground matters as well. Lost Dutchman State Park and Silly Mountain Park are named stretches of desert near the city, and a lot that borders open ground may see animals arrive from there. Tell the technician if yours does so the walk-around covers burrows and brush along the lot line.

Trapping rats safely.

Rats avoid anything new in their path, so placement matters more than the number of traps. Traps go along travel routes in the attic and along walls, and they are covered or boxed so children, pets and wildlife cannot reach them.

We prefer snap traps and tamper-resistant stations over loose bait. Federal rules limit consumer rodenticides to bait stations, and the US EPA page in our sources explains why. Poison can also leave a dead rat in a wall, which becomes an odor problem. Our dead rodent removal page covers that case.

Sealing the roofline.

Rats chew, so sealing uses metal and hardware cloth, not foam or caulk alone. Roof edges, gable and soffit vents, and gaps around plumbing stacks are the usual targets. The work is done from a ladder and sometimes from the roof, so access and weather are part of the plan.

A summer attic is very hot, so work in it is scheduled with that in mind. If the attic needs cleanup after sealing, see attic and crawl space rodent cleanup. For work outside the walls, backyard and outdoor rodent control covers the yard.

Keeping rats from coming back.

Follow-up matters because rats test barriers. A return visit checks the traps, confirms the seals are intact and looks for fresh gnawing at the edges of repairs. Any new activity is easier to deal with in the first week than the third.

Safe handling checklist for droppings and nests

If you plan to clean before a visit, these CDC-based points are the safe way to do it. If you are unsure, wait and ask first.

  • Treat dry droppings as something to dampen first, not something to brush up.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or shed for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Gloves on first, handwashing last. Do both every time.
  • Dampen droppings and urine stains with disinfectant or diluted bleach, leave it for about five minutes, then wipe with paper towels.
  • Put used towels and waste in a plastic bag, tie it closed and throw it away. Never touch a dead rodent without gloves.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Rat control questions

What is the difference between a roof rat and a mouse?

A rat is much larger, with larger droppings, and it needs a bigger opening. Roof rats are agile climbers, so activity often shows up in attics and on rooflines.

Do palms and citrus attract rats?

They can offer food and a climbing route. Fruit left on the ground and thick fronds near a roof are the features that matter most.

Are rats dangerous to touch or clean up after?

Rodent waste can carry germs, so follow CDC cleanup steps and wear gloves. See a doctor if you feel unwell after exposure.

Will trapping end the problem?

It removes the rats that are present. Sealing and yard changes stop new ones from arriving.

Do you handle rats at night?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.
